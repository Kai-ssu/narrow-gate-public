Babylon, 539 BC. The greatest city on earth is feasting.

King Belshazzar has thrown a great banquet for a thousand of his nobles. Wine is poured into the gold and silver goblets that Nebuchadnezzar carried off from the temple of God in Jerusalem, and the guests raise them in praise of gods of gold and silver, bronze, iron, wood and stone.

Outside the walls, the armies of the Medes and Persians are waiting.

You are scribes among the exiles of Judah, keepers of the records of Daniel, who was carried to Babylon as a young man more than sixty years ago. Tonight his archives lie sealed in four locked chests.

Nothing in them is labelled: no book, no chapter, no verse. Only those who know the Scriptures will find their way through, and each archive holds the key to the next, so write down every code you find. When the hand begins to write, it will be too late.
