The year is AD 50. You are traveling with the Apostle Paul and Silas through the Roman colony of Philippi, a proud military outpost filled with pagan worship, Roman law, and economic greed.

For days, as you walked to the place of prayer, a young slave girl possessed by a spirit of prediction followed you, crying out: “These men are servants of the Most High God, who proclaim to you the way of salvation!” Finally Paul, deeply troubled, turned and commanded the spirit in the name of Jesus Christ to leave her. It left her that very hour.

Her owners realized their hope of making money was gone. Enraged, they seized Paul and Silas and dragged them into the marketplace before the Roman rulers. The crowds joined in the attack. The magistrates tore their clothes off them and commanded them to be beaten with rods.

After many blows, Paul and Silas were thrown into the inner prison, their feet secured in painful wooden stocks. The outer doors are bolted. Midnight approaches. The prison is dark, damp, and hopeless.

To break free and witness the power of God tonight, you must work through four stages of testing: confronting the accusation, singing in the darkness, surviving the earthquake, and delivering the message of life to your jailer.
